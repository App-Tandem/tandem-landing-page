Weekly Couple Planning Meeting (30-Minute Template)

A short weekly check-in prevents last-minute stress and keeps both partners aligned on priorities.

Pick one recurring time each week. Sunday evening works for many couples, but any predictable slot is fine. Keep this meeting at 30 minutes so it stays sustainable.

Meeting agenda: 30 minutes total

0-5 min: Wins and context

  • What went well last week?
  • What changed that affects this week?

5-12 min: Calendar alignment

  • Review fixed events (appointments, travel, family obligations).
  • Flag conflict zones and peak-stress days.
  • Schedule relationship time before the week fills up.

12-20 min: Shared tasks and responsibilities

  • Select top 5-7 shared tasks for the week.
  • Assign one owner per task.
  • Set due dates and define what "done" means.

20-26 min: Money check

  • Review expected expenses this week.
  • Confirm any one-off spending decisions.
  • Catch budget risks early.

26-30 min: Commitments recap

  • Repeat top priorities and owners out loud.
  • Confirm next check-in date/time.

Rules that keep the meeting effective

  • Keep it neutral: this is planning, not blame.
  • Limit weekly priorities: fewer items increase completion.
  • Use one shared system for tasks and calendar.
  • Capture decisions immediately to avoid memory-based planning.
If you skip one week, do a 10-minute reset instead of waiting for a "perfect" restart.

FAQ

What is the best day for a weekly couple planning meeting?

Choose the same day every week. Sunday evening and Monday morning are common because they set expectations before the week gets busy.

How long should a couple planning meeting be?

Thirty minutes is enough for alignment without turning planning into another draining task.

What should be included in each meeting?

Review the calendar, select top priorities, assign owners, and align on key spending decisions for the week.
