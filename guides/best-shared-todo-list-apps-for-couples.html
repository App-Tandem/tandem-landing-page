Best Shared To-Do List Apps for Couples in 2026

The right shared list should answer three questions instantly: what needs doing, who owns it, and what changed. We compared five practical options for real couple life.

Published by the Tandem team · Updated September 12, 2026 · How we compared

How we compared the apps

We looked at the workflow a couple uses every week: inviting a partner, capturing a task quickly, assigning one owner, organizing different areas of life, completing tasks from two phones, and keeping plans visible without reminder texts. We also considered whether the app connects tasks to the rest of shared life.

1. Tandem — best all-in-one organizer for couples

Tandem is the best fit when your task list is part of a larger shared life. It is built around one paired relationship rather than a workplace or a generic group. Both partners see shared tasks, can assign clear ownership, organize tasks into lists, set priorities, and complete items from either phone.

The main difference is context. A task like “Book a table for Friday” can sit beside Friday's calendar event, shared spending and the couple's Weekly Sync. That reduces the number of places both people have to remember to check.

2. Apple Reminders — best for an Apple-only household

Apple Reminders is already installed on Apple devices, so it has almost no setup cost for two iPhone users. Shared lists can be used for groceries, chores and errands, and Apple supports assigning reminders to people in a shared list.

Its strength is ecosystem convenience. Its limitation for couples is that it remains a general reminder tool, and a mixed iPhone–Android couple will need another solution.

Feature source: Apple's guide to sharing and assigning reminders.

3. Google Keep — best for a shared checklist with almost no structure

Google Keep is a fast choice for a grocery checklist, packing list or a note both partners can edit. Google says collaborators can edit shared text, lists, images, drawings and audio recordings.

That simplicity is also the tradeoff. Keep is closer to a shared note than a task ownership system. It works well when both people simply tick items off, but less well when recurring household responsibilities need a clear owner and broader context.

Feature source: Google Keep Help: share notes, lists and drawings.

4. Microsoft To Do — best for couples using Microsoft accounts

Microsoft To Do supports sharing a list through an invitation link. Once the other person joins with a Microsoft account, tasks in that shared list can be assigned. This makes it a capable free choice for chores and errands across platforms.

The extra friction is account and ecosystem oriented: both partners need Microsoft accounts, and the product is designed as a personal productivity tool rather than a couple's shared space.

Feature source: Microsoft Support: create and share lists.

5. Todoist — best for couples who want project-management depth

Todoist offers shared projects, task assignment, comments, files, priorities and flexible organization. It is the most powerful option in this comparison for a couple planning a renovation, wedding, move or another project with many steps.

That power can be more structure than everyday couple life needs. Todoist's language and hierarchy are designed for projects and teams, while relationship-specific context still lives elsewhere.

Feature sources: Todoist collaboration for friends and family and Todoist project sharing.

The one-week couple test

Do not choose based on a feature list alone. Test the real behavior you want the app to improve:

  1. Invite your partner and make sure joining works without a setup tutorial.
  2. Create ten real tasks: groceries, one bill, two chores, an errand and one plan for the weekend.
  3. Give every task one owner. Avoid “both” because shared responsibility often becomes unclear responsibility.
  4. Use the app from both phones for seven days.
  5. At the end, ask: did we send fewer reminder messages, and did both people open the app without being prompted?
The best shared to-do app is not the one with the longest feature list. It is the one both partners remember to use when life gets busy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best shared to-do list app for couples?

Tandem is the strongest fit when tasks need to connect with shared calendar plans, expenses and Weekly Sync. Apple Reminders is convenient for an Apple-only couple, and Todoist is a better fit for complex projects.

Can couples assign tasks to each other?

Yes. Tandem, Apple Reminders, Microsoft To Do and Todoist support clear task ownership. Google Keep is better understood as a collaborative checklist.

Should every shared task be assigned?

Assign anything that would otherwise require a reminder or decision. A casual shopping list can stay collaborative, but bills, bookings, errands and household responsibilities benefit from one clear owner.

Do we need a separate list for chores?

Usually yes. Keeping household work separate from groceries, travel and personal tasks makes ownership easier to review. Start with one shared household list before adding more categories.
