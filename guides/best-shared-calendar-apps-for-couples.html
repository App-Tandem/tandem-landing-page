Google Calendar: best for a mixed iPhone and Android couple

Google Calendar is free and works in a browser, on iPhone and on Android. You share a calendar by adding your partner's email, and you choose a permission level: free/busy only, see event details, make changes, or manage sharing. It is a general calendar, so tasks, spending and relationship check-ins live in other apps.

Source: Google Calendar Help: share your calendar.

Apple Calendar: best for two iPhone users

Apple Calendar is already on your phones, and iCloud calendars can be shared. If one of you uses Android, you need a different tool.

Source: Apple: share iCloud calendars.

TimeTree: best for a calendar-only group tool

TimeTree describes itself as "an app for easy calendar sharing and communication". You create a calendar, send an invite, and you can switch between calendars with filters. It has a free plan. Its Premium plan was listed at $4.49 per month or $44.99 per year, with ad-free calendars and file attachments.

Source: timetreeapp.com, checked October 22, 2026.

Tandem: best when the calendar is part of shared life

Tandem: Couples Organizer is made for two paired partners. The calendar shows shared events and your partner's events, with colours, repeating events and reminders. You can also show events from your phone calendar to your partner (they see them read-only). The same app holds shared and private to-do lists, shared expenses with a "who owes who" balance, and a Weekly Sync. Widgets for iOS and Android show upcoming events.

Choose Tandem if: you want one app for two people. Look elsewhere if: you need to share a calendar with more than your partner, or you want a pure calendar with no other features.

One shared calendar or two?

Keep your own calendars for work and personal plans. Add one shared calendar for things that involve both of you: dinners, travel, appointments, visits, bills due. If everything goes in the shared one, you will stop reading it.

Three rules before you start

  1. Who adds events? The person who learns about it adds it, right away.
  2. Colours. One colour per person and one for "us".
  3. Private time. Mark it as busy, with no details, so the other person can plan around it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best shared calendar app for couples?

It depends on your phones and what else you want to share. Google Calendar works across iPhone and Android and is free. Apple Calendar is simplest for two iPhone users. TimeTree is built for sharing calendars with a group. Tandem: Couples Organizer fits couples who want the calendar next to shared to-dos, expenses and a Weekly Sync.

Can an iPhone user and an Android user share a calendar?

Yes. Google Calendar is the most common route because it works in a browser, on iPhone and on Android. TimeTree and Tandem also have iOS and Android apps.

Should we share one calendar or two?

Most couples do best with one shared calendar for joint plans plus each person's own calendar. Put only things that involve both of you in the shared one, so it stays readable.

Is a shared calendar app free?

Google Calendar and TimeTree have free plans. TimeTree lists a Premium plan at $4.49 per month or $44.99 per year (checked October 22, 2026). Tandem is free to start. Prices change, so check each store page.

What should we agree on before sharing a calendar?

Four rules: who adds an event, how you mark private time, what colour means what, and how far ahead you plan. See our guide on shared calendar rules for couples.
